# 18 Nov — the double-check pass

Everything merged for the day (#33–#44) re-read against the code, plus what that pass
found that a single summary cannot show. Three invariants were made mechanical, and
four new holes were closed.

## Invariant 1 — every rejection has a sentence

`_note_reject` now has a `_REJECT_TEXT` entry for every kind the code writes, checked by
walking the AST rather than by eye:

| kind | call sites | summary text |
|---|---|---|
| `earlier` | 2 | fired before their sector was picked |
| `opposite` | 1 | on the opposite side to the pick |
| `no_stop` | 1 | without a valid EMA9 stop |
| `oi` | 1 | not confirmed by open interest |
| `no_data` | 1 | name-checks had no 5m bar from the feed |
| `scan_error` | 2 | could not be scanned |
| `late` | 1 | scanned too late |
| `cap` | 1 | not taken — the pick already had its entries |
| `no_target` | 1 | without a pivot target |

`alert_cap` is the one entry with no writer left; it stays for the 1m scan's alert cap.

`no_data` was also miscounted in words: `147 had no 5m candle…` is 147 **name-checks**
across five candles (one name × one candle per key), and the bar was missing from the
feed, not from the scan. The line now says `147 name-checks had no 5m bar from the feed`,
and — because a one-item list would read `1 name-checks` — the singular is handled.

## Invariant 2 — every cancellation names its gate, in one vocabulary

Bot 1's `Early alerts 1: confirmed 0 · cancelled 1 · unverified 0` had no reason, while
bot 3 printed `Not held: …`. Bot 1 now books each cancellation under a code from bot 3's
own glossary (`scan1m._CANCEL_WHY`) and prints it in bot 3's order, so the two reports
describe the same two projections with the same words:

```
5m:  Not held: no signal on the closed candle 1
1m:  Not held: no signal on the closed candle 2
```

The messages were aligned with it. Bot 1's generic `did not hold at this close` split
into the two deaths it stood for (`no signal on the closed candle`, `the closed candle
fired the opposite side`), each gate's wording now matches bot 3's label, and the
"price helped anyway" clause bot 3 prints is printed here too:

```
❌ EARLY CANCELLED — SELL LGEINDIA (NORMAL SELL) · no signal on the closed candle · 10:15 close
   · close 1712.10 vs early 1714.80 (+0.16%) — price moved in our favour, the signal did not repeat
   · no confirmed entry — manage/exit if you already entered
```

**Two write-offs that used to lie about themselves.** `_settle`'s fallback said
`did not hold` in the message while the summary filed the same projection under
`unchecked` ("could not be checked in time"). The fallback is gone: a write-off with no
gate is `could not be checked in time` in both places, and the two *named* write-offs
(a pick that dropped to WAIT, a sector with no free name) now have their own codes
(`pick_wait`, `no_free`) instead of being pooled with scan errors.

**A slow scan now says so.** A name whose close scan ran past `MAX_ALERT_LAG_SECONDS`
is dropped as `late`; its projection used to be left to the end-of-run sweep and
reported as *unverified*, which cannot tell a slow scan from a broken feed. It is
cancelled inline with `the close scan ran past the alert deadline`.

## Invariant 3 — "Early passes" counts passes that looked

`Early passes 57 · with live 1m data 57` was three ambiguities in one line:

* `early_passes` was bumped at the **end** of a pass, so a pass that returned early —
  the pick sector had no names in the universe, or every name was already alerted or
  held — counted as no pass at all, and the candle silently vanished;
* both early returns are now counted (`Early pass skipped: every name already alerted
  or held 3 · no names in the picked sector 1`), and a pass is counted where it starts
  *looking*;
* `with live 1m data 57` sat beside `Early passes 57` and read as a restatement of the
  names. It counts **passes with at least one live name**, so the line now says
  `all with live 1m data` or `38 of them with live 1m data`.

## What the double check added: the number the day needed

18 Nov asked the catch-up line to explain `catch-ups 0` and could not. The only
lateness figure in the summary was for *entry alerts that were sent* — and none were.
`leader_max_minutes()` is 30, and the youngest refused fire was 34 minutes old, which
means the first check of that pick ran about ten minutes after its candle closed. The
scan's own lag was never measured.

It is now, once per candle (retries of one candle are one measurement; a replayed candle
is never measured), and printed only when it exceeds the deadline:

```
⏱ close scan late: 40 of 72 candles judged more than 180s after their close (slowest 640s)
```

That line is the difference between "the engine found nothing" and "the engine looked at
the wrong minute" — and on 18 Nov both were true at once: every older fire was refused
as `old` (34 min vs a 30 min cap) and no fresh crossover appeared on the picked candles.
