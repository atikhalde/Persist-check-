# 18 Nov — the 5m (bot 1) summary, audited

The same day's report from the other bot, checked line by line against the code that writes it.

```
📊 Scanner summary 2026-11-18
Candles scanned 72 of 75 · with a sector pick 57
Sectors picked (candles): CDUR short 51 · MET short 6
Early passes 57 · with live 1m data 57 · newest 1m bar 73s old on average
Early alerts 1: confirmed 0 · cancelled 1 · unverified 0
Entries 0
Signals not alerted: 20 fired before their sector was picked (CROMPTON 09:25, DIXON 09:25, HAVELLS 09:25, KALYANKJIL 09:20, TITAN 09:25, VOLTAS 09:25 …) · 3 on the opposite side to the pick (AMBER 09:50, BLUESTARCO 09:50, PGEL 09:50) · 147 had no 5m candle to scan (BATAINDIA 09:45, VOLTAS 09:45, WHIRLPOOL 09:45, TITAN 09:45, WHIRLPOOL 09:50, BATAINDIA 10:15 …)
LATE LEADER catch-ups 0 (signal fired before its sector was picked) · not sent: older than the window 963
Open at the close: none — every entry got its exit alert
At the confirm scan: newest 1m bar 45s old  on average
```

Two of these were already fixed on bot 3 and are shared code, so they carry to bot 1 with no
new work: the seven caught names are no longer listed as "not alerted" (`_summary_rejects`
skips `sent ∪ catchup`, and bot 1 does fill `st["catchup"]`), and a zero-entry day prints
`no entry was opened today`. What follows is what was still wrong on this side.

## Finding 1 — `catch-ups 0` against bot 3's `7`, and a counter of 963

The two bots saw the same morning and reported the same class of alert completely differently:

```
5m:  LATE LEADER catch-ups 0 … · not sent: older than the window 963
1m:  LATE LEADER catch-ups 7 … · not sent (per name-check): already ran (chase) 2 · older than the window 48 · no earlier fire 12
```

**963 is 57 picked candles' worth of re-checks, not 963 candidates.** Bot 3 evaluates a
catch-up inside a ten-minute window that opens when a sector is *newly picked*
(`catchup_due`); bot 1 called `catchup_candidate` for every name on **every** candle of the
pick. CDUR was the pick for 51 candles, so 20 names were re-examined 48 times each and each
refusal was counted. Bot 1 now opens the same ten-minute window on the same event
(`_late_due`, keyed on `pick_sector|pick_side`), which puts the day's counter in the same
units as bot 3's: **≈60 name-checks for a 51-candle pick**, not 963.

**Why zero, though.** A window that never closes *cannot* send fewer catch-ups than one that
does — bot 1 was checking at least as often as bot 3, and still sent none. Every one of the 963
checks was refused as `old`, the age guard (`leader_max_minutes`), which compares the clock
with the fire's own candle close. That means at the first candle of the pick the fire's candle
had *already* been closed for longer than the guard: the 5m loop was reading candles tens of
minutes behind the clock that morning — the same lateness that shows up as the one-candle
difference in the header (Finding 4). The refusal count said nothing about that, because the
age at refusal was thrown away. It is now recorded and printed:

```
LATE LEADER catch-ups 0 (signal fired before its sector was picked) · not sent (per name-check): older than the window 62 · youngest refused fire 34 min old
```

`youngest refused fire 34 min old` is the diagnosis in one number: the freshest fire the scan
ever looked at had closed 34 minutes earlier, so no catch-up was possible — and it points at
the loop's clock rather than at the names. Both bots print it.

## Finding 2 — `cancelled 1` and no reason

`Early alerts 1: confirmed 0 · cancelled 1 · unverified 0` — one projection died on 18 Nov and
bot 1's summary never said which gate killed it, while bot 3 printed `did not hold` plus a
`Not held: …` breakdown for its two. Bot 1 already carried the reason: it is the clause in the
`❌ EARLY CANCELLED` message. The cancellation is now bucketed by that same clause, so the two
cannot drift:

```
Early alerts 1: confirmed 0 · cancelled 1 · unverified 0
Not held: did not hold at this close 1
```

An `unverified` heads-up (no close ever came) is *not* a cancellation and is not counted there.

**Silent passes, too.** `run_early` returned `[]` for three unrelated reasons — the loop
reached the candle after it closed, there was no pick on the previous candle, or the pass ran
and found nothing — and `Early passes 57` looked identical in all three. The first two are now
counted and printed:

```
Early pass skipped: after the candle closed 3 · no pick on the previous candle 1
```

That line is what will explain a day like this one: bot 1 projected **one** name where bot 3
projected two, and there was no way to see whether a pass had been skipped, been too late, or
simply found nothing.

## Finding 3 — `147 had no 5m candle to scan` was neither 147 names nor a candle problem

`no_data` is keyed by `name@candle`, so the count is name-checks: 147 across five candles
(09:45, 09:50, 10:15), roughly 20 names × 5 candles with the misses. The names shown —
BATAINDIA, VOLTAS, WHIRLPOOL, TITAN — are the most liquid in the sector, so nothing was
suspended; Yahoo simply published no completed bar for that name on that candle. Both facts now
appear in the text, in the order a reader needs them:

```
· 147 name-checks had no 5m bar from the feed (BATAINDIA 09:45, VOLTAS 09:45, …)
```

The other per-candle counters (`late`, `no_stop`, `no_target`, `oi`, `cap`) are keyed the same
way but stayed small enough to read as names on this day; they are left alone rather than
lengthen every clause.

## Finding 4 — the two headers differ by one candle, and one pick

```
5m:  Candles scanned 72 of 75 · with a sector pick 57 · CDUR short 51 · MET short 6
1m:  Candles confirmed 73 of 75 · with a sector pick 57 · CDUR short 50 · MET short 7
```

Not a contradiction, and deliberately not "fixed" to match: the verbs now say what each counts
(candles the close pass *judged* vs candles the 1m loop *confirmed*), and a candle at a
boundary can be on one side of the clock and not the other — the same boundary effect that
moved CDUR/MET by one. The totals agree on both: 51 + 6 = 57 = 50 + 7 = `with a sector pick 57`,
and both bots ran 57 picked candles.

## Finding 5 — the double space

`newest 1m bar 45s old  on average` — the line already formats with a single space in the code
that ships; the day's paste was from an older build. A test now pins the exact string so it
cannot come back.

## Still open

* **Why bot 1 projected only one name to bot 3's two** (LGEINDIA 10:16 is the one it sent;
  BATAINDIA 10:52 is the one it did not). The new `Early pass skipped:` line will name the
  reason next time it happens, but for 18 Nov it needs the 10:45–10:55 blocks or a log upload.
* **Why the 5m loop was running behind the clock** (Finding 1) — the mechanism is visible
  (every refusal was `old`, youngest 34 min), the cause is in that day's loop timing and is
  not recoverable from the summary alone.

## Tests

`tests/test_scan1m.py::LateWindowTests` (3) and `::Bot1SummaryTests` (6), plus 3 in
`tests/test_early_alerts.py`:

* the catch-up window opens with the pick, holds for ten minutes and re-opens on a new pick,
* a refusal records the age that refused it (09:30 fire → 65 min at a 10:40 clock),
* both bots print `youngest refused fire 34 min old`,
* a cancellation names its gate, taken from the message it sent; an unverified is not one,
* `147 name-checks had no 5m bar from the feed`, with the names and candles intact,
* the confirm-scan line has one space,
* a pass that arrived after the close, or with no previous pick, is counted; a pass that looked
  and found nothing is not.

Full suite: **577 passed**, pyflakes and yamllint clean. No entry/exit rule, picker threshold or
`_dir_conf` leg was touched: the catch-up window changes *how often a refusal is checked*, not
what counts as a fresh fire (`leader_max_minutes` is untouched), and the rest is text, counters
and one new reason line.
