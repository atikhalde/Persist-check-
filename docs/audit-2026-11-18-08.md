# 18 Nov — the 5m log, read at last

The morning loop's own log (run **#1771**, 08:30–11:25 IST) settled both open questions and
found the reason the audit was stuck: **eight alerts of that morning were never delivered.**

## Finding 1 — Telegram refused eight messages, and nothing said so twice

Four of them are the blocks the audit has been asking for since `audit-2026-11-18-03.md` §7.

```
09:20:13 WARNING nse_scanner.telegram: telegram primary send failed: 400
  {"ok":false,"error_code":400,"description":"Bad Request: can't parse entities:
   Unsupported start tag \"\" at byte offset 400"}
… (three attempts, then)
09:20:17 ERROR nse_scanner.telegram: telegram primary delivery failed: …
```

That is `Bad Request: can't parse entities: Unsupported start tag ""` — Telegram's way of
saying it met a **bare `<`**. The reason lines carry one:

```python
# nse_scanner/market.py
f"WAIT — market {regime} conf {dir_conf} < {PICK_MIN_CONF}"     # "conf 30 < 55"
f"{s.name} (persist {score:.0f} < {floor:.0f})"                 # "persist 43 < 50"
```

Telegram parses the message as HTML; `< 55` opens a tag whose name is a space, and the whole
message is rejected. Two guards now:

* **escape** — `telegram.esc()` on the free text that reaches a message (the pick reason and
  the signal reason): `conf 30 &lt; 55` renders as `conf 30 < 55`;
* **never lose the words** — if Telegram still rejects the markup, the same message is re-sent
  once **without** `parse_mode` and with the scanner's own tags stripped. The reader loses the
  bold, not the warning.

Eight candles of that morning were affected — `09:15, 09:20, 09:25, 09:30, 09:40, 10:00,
10:05, 10:10` — eleven attempted sends in all (the 10:15 block was attempted three times,
once per retry pass, because a failed send leaves the block unsent in the ledger). The four
WAIT blocks the audit kept asking for are among them. They were never missing from the code;
they were being eaten by the messenger.

## Finding 2 — which gate held 09:15–09:40: the opening range

The log prints the decision every candle:

| candle | label | conf | pick |
|---|---|---|---|
| 09:15 | NEUTRAL | 30 | WAIT |
| 09:20 | NEUTRAL | 40 | WAIT |
| 09:25 | NEUTRAL | 44 | WAIT |
| 09:30 | NEUTRAL | 51 | WAIT |
| 09:35 | — | — | *candle abandoned: no Nifty bar* |
| 09:40 | **NEUTRAL** | **68** | WAIT |
| 09:45 | **BEAR** | **68** | **CDUR** |
| 10:00 | BEAR | 43 | WAIT |
| 10:05 | BEAR | 38 | WAIT |
| 10:10 | BEAR | 38 | WAIT |
| 10:15 | BEAR | 76 | CDUR |

The first four candles fail *both* tests — confidence below `PICK_MIN_CONF` (55) and no
direction. **09:40 is the decisive one: confidence 68, already above the threshold, and the
pick is still WAIT because the label is NEUTRAL.** Confidence does not move at 09:45 (68
before, 68 after) — only the label does, and the pick appears in the same breath.

That is the opening-range gate: `OR_MINUTES = 30`, "opening range 09:15–09:45"
(`session.py`), and `market.py` disables the directional legs while `mom < OR_MINUTES`. So
09:15–09:40 was not CHOP, not persistence and not the picker: the session's first 30 minutes
had not finished, so there was no direction to pick with.

The 10:00–10:10 WAIT is the *other* gate in action — direction BEAR, confidence 43/38/38
below 55. One day, both gates, now visible.

This also answers the chart question: the TradingView indicator fires CDUR short at 09:20
(FLOW) and 09:30 (SECTOR) while the scanner waits for 09:45 — by design, not by error.

## Finding 3 — why bot 1 projected one name and bot 3 projected two

```
10:15 early pass: "no current sector pick from 10:10 — skipped"    (10:10 was WAIT)
10:50 early pass: 12 names, 12 with live 1m data, 1 alert(s), 50s before close
      EARLY SELL-EX5 BATAINDIA price=627.25 sl=629.32 @ 10:50 (sent 10:54:01 IST)
```

Bot 1's single projection is **BATAINDIA 10:50**. Bot 3's second one is **LGEINDIA 10:15**,
and bot 1 could not have produced it: its early pass scans the sector picked by the
**previous completed** candle, and the 10:10 candle was WAIT, so the 10:15 pass had nothing
to scan. Bot 3 projects from the **forming** candle's own pick — that is what "projected"
means on that bot — so it found LGEINDIA anyway. Two designs, both working; not a missing
alert. The log also shows bot 1 *seeing* that fire later (`LGEINDIA fires=NORMAL SELL@10:15`
at 10:45) and classifying it as pre-pick, which is what the LATE LEADER rule is for.

Also visible: `10:25 … 1 with live 1m data`, `10:35 … 1 with live 1m data` — the 1m feed was
nearly dead for two passes. That is the "with live 1m data" unit the summary now spells out.

## Finding 6 — the log is unreadable because of a secret

Every `1` in the log body is `***` (`conf=5***` = 51, `09:***5` = 09:15, `(***2 names)` = 12
names) while the Actions timestamp keeps its digits. GitHub masks secret values in logs, so
**one of the repository's secrets has a one-character value made of digits** — likely a stale
`TELEGRAM_CHAT_ID`-style secret holding `1`. It will keep corrupting every log until it is
deleted. (This is why the quotes above show `2026-1-18` in place of `2026-11-18`.)
